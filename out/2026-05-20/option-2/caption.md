# Option 2 — Meta cut 8,000 people and raised spend by $30B — same day

**Source:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/20/meta-job-cuts-10-percent-8000-employees/

---

Meta filed a single SEC 8-K this morning that cut 8,000 jobs and raised its 2026 capex ceiling to $145 billion. Not two separate announcements. One filing.

That is not a coincidence. It is a strategy made legible by an accountant.

Meta is profitable. Its ad revenue is growing. This is not a distressed company finding savings — it is a healthy company making a deliberate allocation decision: human operating cost out, AI compute infrastructure in. The capex range jumped from $115-135B to $125-145B explicitly to fund AI data centers and compute capacity.

The 8,000 layoffs will dominate headlines because human displacement is concrete and emotionally legible. But the $30B capex increase is the structural signal worth sitting with. It tells you that Meta's leadership has concluded, with enough conviction to publish it via SEC disclosure, that AI infrastructure spending at this scale will compound into durable competitive advantage.

When a profitable, growing company makes this trade publicly, it gives every other CFO in tech a data point and a model to run.

The question is not whether other companies will run the same analysis. They will. The question is whether the productivity data actually holds — and what gets cut when it does not.

Is your organization already running this calculation? And if not, who owns the decision when it lands on your desk?
