# Viral watch — source catalog

This catalog is tuned for detecting **cross-media surge** on stories with
implications for **workforce/labor** and **culture, equity & values**. It is
broader than the `/weekly-summary` catalog — viral stories often start in
culture/labor outlets before reaching business news, and they often surge
across general-audience publications that don't usually cover enterprise.

## Surge bar (cross-citation rule)

A story qualifies as "viral" when it meets ALL of:

1. **≥3 catalog outlets** cover it within a **48-72 hour cluster**.
2. **≥1 of those outlets is from a Tier-1 group below** (Tier-1 global news,
   Tier-1 public broadcasters, or a flagship culture/labor magazine of record).
3. The story carries a specific implication for **workforce/labor** or
   **culture, equity & values**.

Slow-burn coverage that accumulates over a full week without a 48-72h cluster
does NOT qualify. The surge window is what makes it viral.

## Do not count — excluded sources

These show up in viral-story search results but **must not contribute** to the
cross-citation count:

- **Aggregator / repost sites:** Slashdot, BoredPanda, BroBible, UNILAD,
  LADbible, Daily Mail (repost-heavy), Yahoo News (when reposting AP/Reuters —
  count the original once)
- **Engagement-farm / clickbait outlets:** Buzzfeed (post-news era, when
  unsourced), New York Post (verify primary source independently — many
  tabloid framings)
- **Crypto / hype blogs:** MEXC News, OneKey Blog, cryptobriefing
- **AI content farms:** mean.ceo, llm-stats.com, releasebot.io, ai2.work,
  finalroundai, AICC, originbrief
- **Partisan opinion-as-news outlets:** Breitbart, The Daily Caller,
  HuffPost opinion, Salon opinion (use ONLY for documenting "framing observed
  in X audience", never as a fact source)
- **Personal blogs / Substacks** (unless from a recognized analyst already
  listed in the catalog as Tier-1 thought leadership)
- **X/Twitter, Reddit, TikTok, Instagram posts** — count as **leads** for
  finding the surge, never as the citation. Always trace to the underlying
  primary source.

When one of these appears in search results, **trace to the primary source**
in the catalog above and cite that.

## Framing-coverage instruction

Viral stories almost always carry contested framings. When researching, the
skill should actively look for the **distinct audience reads** of the same
story:

- **Mainstream / centrist framing** — typically captured by the Tier-1
  global news group above.
- **Left-leaning framing** — Vox, The Atlantic, NYT opinion, Guardian
  comment, NPR.
- **Right-leaning framing** — WSJ editorial, Fox News, NY Post, National
  Review. (Use Fox News / NY Post as **framing evidence**, not fact
  sources.)
- **Workforce / labor framing** — HBR, MIT Sloan, Labor Notes, Brookings.
- **Online / internet-native framing** — Platformer, 404 Media, Garbage Day,
  The Verge.
- **Affected-community framing** — when the story is about a specific
  demographic, find at least one outlet that centers that community's read
  (e.g. The 19th for women; Capital B for Black audiences; Documented for
  immigrant communities; NewsHour Indian Country for Indigenous; Them for
  LGBTQ+).
- **SEA-specific framings** — when the story originates in or affects SEA,
  capture the relevant national framing in the country's flagship outlet
  (e.g. Inquirer for Filipino audience reads, Jakarta Post for Indonesian,
  Bangkok Post for Thai, VnExpress for Vietnamese). Watch for divergence
  between **ASEAN-government framing** (CNA, Straits Times, official press)
  vs. **independent / migrant-worker / labor framing** (Tempo, Rappler, ILO
  Asia-Pacific, ETHRWorld SEA). Religion (Islam in Indonesia/Malaysia/Brunei,
  Buddhism in Thailand/Myanmar, Catholicism in the Philippines) and ethnic
  identity often produce sharper local framings than the English-language
  regional press surfaces.

Capture framings as **observed facts**, not as the brief's positions. The
brief is sober; the framings are what is happening around the story.

## Discovery rules

The skill MAY add a new source to **Auto-discovered sources** below when ALL
of the following are true:

1. The source published a contributing story inside the resolved window.
2. The source is a recognized newsroom, magazine, public broadcaster,
   academic / think-tank institution, or affected-community publication of
   record — not on the exclusion list above.
3. The story is independently cross-cited by at least one Tier-1 catalog
   source already (the new source is corroborating, not the sole citation).
4. The source is reachable via WebFetch.

When you add a discovered source, mention it in the final report.
