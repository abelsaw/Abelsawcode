There's a new shape to AI releases. It looks like a funnel.

At the top, every frontier model still gets built the way it always has — at OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, xAI, and whoever's next. At the bottom, what reaches the market is a narrower, slower, federally-cleared subset. In between sits the June 2 Executive Order on pre-release model evaluation, and the federal-review pipeline it activated.

Two labs walked through that funnel on Friday June 26. OpenAI shipped GPT-5.6 (Sol / Terra / Luna) to ~20 US-government-approved organizations. Anthropic shipped Mythos 5 to 100+ US institutions. Same day, same review pattern, same narrow exit.

This isn't a one-off compliance moment. The same federal layer is operating across the tech stack the same week — CISA issued an emergency directive on Check Point VPN zero-day CVE-2026-50751. The Senate Banking Committee is preparing sanctions against Alibaba/Qwen-linked actors after Anthropic's 28.8M-exchange distillation letter. Europe is building the mirror move: NVIDIA at ISC announced 35 new EU AI supercomputers — sovereign infrastructure as the EU's industrial-policy unit.

What changes for me as a CTO:

1. Frontier-model procurement now has a clearance-lag dependency. Plan multi-week gates into any Sol- or Mythos-class workload roadmap.

2. The funnel rewards inference cost. OpenAI taped out Jalapeño with Broadcom the same week — ~50% cheaper than GPUs. Cheaper compute is what makes a slower release schedule survivable.

3. The pipeline is now the product. The question is no longer "which model" — it's "which cleared tier of which model, on what review tempo."

Frontier capability is no longer a flat ladder. It's a funnel with a federal aperture at the bottom.
