Ten stories from Singapore and Malaysia defined the week. Coalition stress in Johor. An SPF anti-xenophobia takedown. A senior minister back to surgery. A wage-floor cliff in July. Plus Sarawak's federalism question. Ranked by catalog citations.

1. Johor Assembly dissolved June 1. Snap election within 60 days. Barisan Nasional contests all 56 seats without Pakatan Harapan. Anwar's Unity Government faces its first real coalition test; Negeri Sembilan signaling parallel polls.

2. Singapore Police issued Disabling Directions to YouTube, Facebook and X under OCHA 2023 over 14 posts targeting the Indian community. Investigations traced content to a China-based platform. Singapore's multiracial policy got institutional backing.

3. Senior Minister of State Koh Poh Koon stepped down effective June 1, relinquishing Manpower and Health. He returns to surgery. A politician choosing medicine and family is its own signal about the cost of public office.

4. At the ASEAN U19 in Medan, a Malaysian player kicked a Singaporean opponent in the chest. The Vietnamese referee gave only a verbal warning. Three federations now have a discipline conversation to host.

5. Singaporean Francis Tan was handed over by Royal Malaysia Police to SPF June 5 over the Cambodia scam ring tied to 535 victims and S$53M losses. Thirty of 34 suspects still at large.

6. Singapore's Local Qualifying Salary rises to S$1,800 from July 1. SMEs face foreign-worker quota recalculation. PWCS co-funds 30% of qualifying raises through 2028. Plan renewals now.

7. The Petronas–Sarawak question returned. Sabah's High Court win on revenue and Sarawak's Petros aggregator bid are reshaping Malaysian federalism. Not an oil story; a state-federal question.

8. The ringgit strengthened to 3.97 per dollar, highest since June 2018, on optimism about Malaysia's AI supply chain role. Currency strength matters for anyone running regional payroll.

9. HDB launched 6,860 new flats across seven BTO projects in June, including Lakeview and Shunfu — first time in over four decades for those areas. The housing pipeline is the retention story SG employers are watching.

10. PM Anwar told the Energy Transition Conference his federal government stays focused on its agenda, not Johor polls. Federal policy continues regardless of state weather. Read it as steadying the macro story.

What I am carrying into next week: Singapore and Malaysia's accountability stories are converging. The SPF anti-Indian takedown and Sarawak's federalism question ask the same thing — who defines the national story? For leaders running MY-SG operations, the answer affects hiring, public stance, and trust. Where is that conversation in your business?
