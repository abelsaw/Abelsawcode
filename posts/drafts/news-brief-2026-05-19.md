## 1. April US job cuts up 38% MoM; AI now top driver for second straight month
- **Topic:** Talent & workforce trends / AI in HR
- **Source:** Challenger, Gray & Christmas monthly report; coverage in HR Dive, CBS News, HR Executive, CFO Dive
- **URL:** https://www.hrdive.com/news/tech-layoffs-rise-ai-remains-top-driver-us-workforce-cuts/819698/
- **Also covered by:** https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ai-layoffs-job-cuts-challenger-report-april-2026/ · https://hrexecutive.com/challenger-april-job-cuts-surged-38-whats-next/ · primary: https://www.challengergray.com/blog/challenger-report-april-job-cuts-rise-38-from-march-ytd-cuts-down-50/
- **Date:** Released early May 2026 (April data)
- **What happened:** US employers announced 83,387 job cuts in April — the third-highest April total ever, up 38% from March's 60,620. AI led all cited reasons for the second consecutive month, named in 21,490 cuts (26% of April total, 49,135 YTD). Tech sector alone announced 33,361 cuts in April; YTD tech cuts of 85,411 are up 33% YoY. Andy Challenger (CRO): "Regardless of whether individual jobs are being replaced by AI, the money for those roles is."
- **Why a CHRO cares:** AI-attributed reductions have moved from edge cases to the dominant published reason for cuts. That changes how HR talks about workforce planning, severance comms, and reskilling investment cases.
- **Angle ideas:**
  - "Money for the roles, not just the roles" — the CFO framing CHROs need to be ready for.
  - The honesty problem: when "AI" is the cited reason for cuts that aren't really AI-driven.
  - Why YTD cuts being down 50% YoY doesn't soften the AI signal.

## 2. Meta and Microsoft announce ~20,000 combined cuts; both cite AI
- **Topic:** Talent & workforce trends / AI in HR
- **Source:** CNBC, Fortune, Invezz, Deccan Herald
- **URL:** https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/24/20k-job-cuts-at-meta-microsoft-raise-concern-of-ai-labor-crisis-.html
- **Also covered by:** https://fortune.com/2026/04/23/meta-microsoft-layoffs-job-cuts-not-filling-open-roles-voluntary-buyouts/ · https://invezz.com/news/2026/05/04/is-big-techs-725b-ai-splurge-being-funded-by-mass-layoffs/
- **Date:** April 23-24, 2026 announcements; May 4 analysis
- **What happened:** Meta plans to cut ~8,000 jobs (10% of workforce) starting May 20, plus leaving ~6,000 vacancies unfilled. Microsoft is offering buyouts to ~8,750 US employees (~7% of US staff) whose age + years of service totals 70+, excluding some senior and sales-incentive roles. Both firms explicitly cited AI automation in content moderation, customer support, software testing, and certain engineering work. Alphabet, Microsoft, Meta and Amazon are collectively projected to spend ~$700B on AI infrastructure in 2026.
- **Why a CHRO cares:** This is the cleanest example to date of headcount funding shifting from people to compute. Sets a template — and a comms challenge — every CHRO outside Big Tech will be asked about.
- **Angle ideas:**
  - "Compute is the new headcount line item" — the budget conversation that's coming for every CFO/CHRO partnership.
  - Why the age-plus-tenure buyout formula at Microsoft is more interesting than the headline number.
  - What the not-filling-vacancies signal means for internal mobility and career paths.

## 3. Mobley v. Workday — court rules ADEA covers job applicants
- **Topic:** AI in HR / Employment law
- **Source:** Maynard Nexsen, AI Governance for HR, National Law Review
- **URL:** https://www.maynardnexsen.com/publication-emerging-liability-for-ai-driven-hiring-tools-key-developments-in-mobley-v-workday-inc
- **Also covered by:** https://www.aigovernanceforhr.com/p/the-mobley-v-workday-case-didnt-end · https://aicompliancedocuments.com/blog/workday-ai-hiring-lawsuit-employer-liability
- **Date:** March 6, 2026 ruling; March 30, 2026 amended complaint
- **What happened:** N.D. Cal. ruled the Age Discrimination in Employment Act protects job applicants subjected to AI hiring screens, rejecting Workday's last major dismissal argument. The opt-in window for the ADEA collective closed March 7, 2026. Workday disclosed in earlier filings that approximately 1.1 billion job applications were rejected via its tools during the relevant period (Sept 2020-present). The case is now positioned to expose not just Workday but the 10,000+ employers using its hiring tools.
- **Why a CHRO cares:** First major AI-hiring-bias case to clear procedural hurdles at scale. Vendor-indemnification clauses, audit logs, and bias-testing documentation just became board-level questions.
- **Angle ideas:**
  - "1.1 billion rejections" — the number every CHRO should be asking their TA tech vendor about.
  - What "joint and several" exposure with your ATS vendor actually means.
  - The audit you should already have run by Q3.

## 4. Mercer: 2026 health benefit costs to rise 6.5% — highest jump since 2010
- **Topic:** Compensation, benefits & wellbeing
- **Source:** SHRM, Mercer, HR Dive, Employee Benefit News
- **URL:** https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/benefits-compensation/employers-brace-15-year-high-health-benefit-cost-hike
- **Also covered by:** https://www.mercer.com/en-us/about/newsroom/employers-are-bracing-for-the-highest-health-benefit-cost-increase-in-15-years/ · https://www.hrdive.com/news/health-benefit-cost-increases-to-hit-15-year-high-mercer/759639/
- **Date:** Spring 2026 (Mercer National Survey of Employer-Sponsored Health Plans, projections release)
- **What happened:** Mercer's survey of ~1,700 employers projects total health benefit cost per employee will rise 6.5% on average in 2026 — the largest increase since 2010 — and that's after planned cost-cutting changes. Without changes, the average would near 9%. Per-employee cost is projected to exceed $18,500. Employee paycheck deductions are expected to rise 6-7%. Fifty-nine percent of employers say they will make plan changes, up from 48% in 2025 and 44% in 2024.
- **Why a CHRO cares:** Forces an explicit choice — pass through to employees, eat the cost, or restructure plan design. All three have retention and engagement consequences. Open enrollment comms strategy starts now, not in Q3.
- **Angle ideas:**
  - The 6-7% paycheck-deduction increase nobody is talking about yet.
  - "Cost-shift fatigue" — why 2026 isn't 2024; employees have lower tolerance after three years of stacked increases.
  - Plan design as a retention lever, not just a cost lever.

## 5. FTC orders Rollins to drop noncompetes covering 18,000+ employees
- **Topic:** DEI, culture & employment law
- **Source:** FTC press release; Federal Newswire, Concurrences, Washington Times, multiple law firm bulletins
- **URL:** https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/04/ftc-takes-action-against-noncompete-agreements-securing-protections-workers
- **Also covered by:** https://thefederalnewswire.com/stories/681428235-ftc-orders-rollins-to-end-noncompete-agreements-for-over-18-000-employees · https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260429-ftc-noncompete-enforcement
- **Date:** April 15, 2026 (proposed consent order)
- **What happened:** FTC issued a proposed consent order requiring Rollins (parent of Orkin, HomeTeam, Critter Control) to stop entering, maintaining, or enforcing post-employment noncompetes against more than 18,000 current and former employees nationwide for 10 years, and to notify those employees they are released. Rollins' agreements typically barred pest-control employment within a 75-mile radius of any of 700+ locations for two years. The FTC sent warning letters to 13 other pest-control firms.
- **Why a CHRO cares:** The 2024 noncompete rule was blocked, so the FTC is now litigating case-by-case. If you've still got broad noncompetes on rank-and-file workers, you're in the target pattern. Counsel review window is now.
- **Angle ideas:**
  - The death of broad noncompetes — not by rule, by enforcement.
  - The warning-letter signal: 13 other companies put on notice without being named.
  - What "reasonable scope" actually looks like in 2026.

## 7. NLRB rules Google a joint employer with Accenture Flex; orders bargaining
- **Topic:** Employment law / culture
- **Source:** SHRM, Bloomberg Law, NLRB
- **URL:** https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/employment-law-compliance/google-will-appeal-nlrb-joint-employer-ruling
- **Also covered by:** https://news.bloomberglaw.com/daily-labor-report/google-can-challenge-joint-employer-status-after-nlrb-ruling · https://www.nlrbedge.com/p/04092026-google-ordered-to-bargain
- **Date:** April 8, 2026
- **What happened:** The NLRB ruled Google a joint employer with staffing firm Accenture Flex regarding content-moderation contract workers for Google Search and help operations, and ordered Google to bargain with Alphabet Workers Union-CWA Local 9009. Workers are fully remote across the US. Google said it will appeal to the Ninth Circuit and argued it stopped setting wage/benefit standards in July 2024; the Board rejected the timing of that argument.
- **Why a CHRO cares:** Even a "we don't control them" position can land you at a bargaining table if your contracts or operational practices give you enough leverage over staffing-firm workers. Time to audit MSAs and day-to-day controls with major staffing partners.
- **Angle ideas:**
  - "You may be a joint employer and not know it" — the contract clauses that flip the determination.
  - The remote-work twist: distributed contractors don't make joint-employment go away.
  - Three questions to ask procurement before the next staffing renewal.
