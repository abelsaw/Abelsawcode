## Option A — Measured

The headline says Sam Altman won. The ruling says something more interesting.

On May 18, a federal court in California dismissed Elon Musk's lawsuit against OpenAI and Altman. A nine-member advisory jury took under two hours to decide Musk had waited too long — his claims fell outside the three-year statute of limitations. Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers accepted the verdict and closed the case.

Read the fine print. The court made no finding on the question everyone actually cares about: did OpenAI abandon its founding promise — a non-profit built to ensure AGI benefits humanity — when it built a commercial engine on top?

That question wasn't answered. It was timed out.

This is the part worth sitting with as we lead our own transformations. The hardest questions in any mission-driven organisation — when does evolution become drift, who owns the original promise, what happens when purpose meets capital — rarely get resolved on principle. They get resolved on procedure. On timing. On who moved first, and who moved too late.

Musk co-founded and funded OpenAI, left the board in 2018, and sued in 2024. The merits of his argument were never tested. The clock decided it.

He says he'll appeal. The mission debate continues — in courtrooms, in boardrooms, and in the gap between what an organisation was founded to do and what it becomes.

Two things I'm carrying from this:

Build your mission into your governance, not just your founding story.

And act on conviction early — because rights you don't exercise in time can quietly expire.

When purpose and commercial reality collide in your organisation, who holds the line — and is it written down anywhere?

## Option B — Bold

Elon Musk just lost to OpenAI. Read past the scoreboard.

On May 18, a California federal court dismissed his case against OpenAI and Sam Altman. Not because he was wrong. A nine-member advisory jury decided he was late — his claims fell outside the three-year statute of limitations. Under two hours of deliberation. Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers accepted it and closed the case.

So the biggest question in AI governance — did OpenAI walk away from its promise to build AGI for humanity when it bolted on a for-profit engine — never went to trial. It expired.

Here's my blunt take.

In transformation, being right is not enough. Being right on time is the whole game.

Every mission-driven organisation sits on the same fault line: purpose on one side, capital on the other. The moment you stop writing the mission into your governance — the charter, the board, the decision rights — you are trusting a founding story to hold a line that only structure can hold.

Musk helped start OpenAI, funded it, left the board in 2018, then waited until 2024 to fight. The merits never mattered. The calendar did.

He will appeal. But the lesson is already on the table.

Protect your mission with governance, not nostalgia. And when conviction says move, the window is shorter than you think.

What would quietly expire in your organisation if you waited two years to defend it?
