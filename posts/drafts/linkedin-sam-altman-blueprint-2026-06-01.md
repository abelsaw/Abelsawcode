## Draft (231 words)

There are founders. And then there are people who reset an entire industry's clock.

Sam Altman's arc is instructive. Stanford at 20, then gone — out to build. He founded Loopt in 2005, sold it to Green Dot by 2012, then joined Y Combinator and became its president in 2014, shaping a generation of startups from the most powerful perch in tech.

He walked away from it. In 2015 he co-founded OpenAI to chase general-purpose AI — a mission most thought was either decades away or naive. CEO by 2019. Then, in 2022, ChatGPT.

The world changed in a weekend. Generative AI went from research curiosity to hundreds of millions of users, and every boardroom on earth suddenly had an AI agenda.

That conviction was tested. In November 2023 his own board removed him. Four days later he was back — reinstated after nearly all 800 employees threatened to follow him out the door. They believed in the mission more than the org chart.

Most leaders optimise the seat they hold. The rare ones leave a safe, powerful position to bet on something unproven — and find out, under fire, who actually believes.

The biggest transformation bets look irrational right up until they redefine the category.

What would you walk away from your strongest position to build?
