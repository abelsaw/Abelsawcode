## 1. Mercer — Global Talent Trends 2026

**Methodology:** ~12,000 respondents — C-suite, HR leaders, employees, investors. Theme: "Driving exponential performance: Solving the human-machine equation."

**Headline findings:**
- **63%** of executives believe redesigning work for AI is a major value driver; only **32%** believe their workforce can combine human and machine work effectively.
- **54%** C-suite say talent scarcity is the top concern; **59%** of HR leaders cite difficulty attracting talent with vital digital skills.
- **98%** of executives planning organization redesign; **99%** expect AI to drive significant change.
- **44%** of employees report thriving at work — **down from 66% in 2024** (worse than pandemic levels).
- **35%** of employees would consider leaving if they felt disadvantaged by unequal access to AI.
- AI job-loss concern jumped from **28% (2024) to 40% (2026)** among employees.
- **62%** of employees believe leaders underestimate AI's emotional impact; only **19%** of HR leaders factor it into digital strategy.
- **8%** of C-suite see HR as an embedded strategic function today.
- **57%** of C-suite see improving people analytics as a priority; only **27%** of executives believe their HR team effectively advises on talent decisions.
- **55%** of executives say their organization underutilizes the workforce intelligence it has.
- **95%** of organizations are getting zero return on AI investments (referencing $30-40B enterprise spend).
- **35%** of employees would trade a 10% pay cut for stronger career development.
- **56%** of HR leaders expect HR and IT to merge; **60%** of C-suite agree HR and IT must merge.
- **47%** of executives say talent intelligence is a priority but only **35%** of HR leaders believe their talent strategy is data-driven.
- **30%** have a skills-based talent strategy; only **26%** of employees say lateral moves are encouraged.
- High-growth firms (10%+ growth) are **2x more likely** to believe acting on talent intelligence drives competitive advantage.

## 2. Mercer — 2025/2026 Skills Snapshot Survey

**Methodology:** HR-leader self-rated effectiveness on 10 key skills practices, scored into high/average/low groups (top ~20%).

**Headline findings:**
- **38%** of companies now use a single enterprise-wide skills library, up from **30% in 2023**.
- **55%** map skills to jobs, **nearly double the 28% in 2022**.
- **40%** map skills to individuals (up from 28% in 2024).
- **43%** are very confident in knowing critical skills (up from 35% in 2023).
- **25%** update skills data continuously (up from 18% in 2023); **49%** at least annually (up from 42%).
- **9%** fail to see impact from AI in skills work — **down dramatically from 45% in 2023**.
- **20%** have a skills-based reward program (up from 17%).
- **50%** have defined an ideal number of skills per job (up from 30% in 2023); the strongest consensus is **10 or fewer skills per role**.
- **53%** still don't manage skill proficiencies at all.
- **61%** still rely on Excel or internally built databases; only **11%** use vendor solutions.
- High-effectiveness organizations are **2.1–2.9x more likely** to maintain a unified skills library and centrally manage proficiencies.
- Top desired benefits of investing in skills practices: attracting/retaining critical skills (1), driving workforce productivity (2).
- **40%** cite organizational change overload as the main barrier (up from 36% in 2023).

*Correction to earlier search-cited number:* Prior posts cited "<5% of HR leaders advanced on skills tools" — the actual figure is approximately **20–24% of organizations rank themselves as highly effective**. The "<5%" figure does not appear in the PDF. Future drafts should use 23–24% as the high-effectiveness rate.

## 3. Deloitte — 2026 Global Human Capital Trends

**Methodology:** Surveyed 6,000+ workers + executives. Theme: "From tensions to tipping points: Choosing the human advantage."

**Headline findings:**
- **7 in 10 business leaders** say their primary competitive edge is differentiation through adaptivity, creativity, and judgment.
- **59%** of organizations take a tech-focused approach to AI — and are **1.6x more likely to not realize returns** on AI investments.
- **60%** of workers now use AI intentionally at work; only **14%** of leaders say they're adept at shaping human-AI interactions.
- **6%** of leaders say they're "making great progress" on designing human-machine interaction (66% recognize importance, 57% have efforts underway).
- One European telecom: 90% of AI budget spent on workflow redesign + training = **30% productivity lift**; 5% spent on tools alone = only **5% lift**.
- **5%** making great progress on AI's implications for decision-making (64% recognize, 62% efforts underway).
- **5%** making great progress on culture/AI alignment (57% recognize).
- **5%** making great progress on workforce data trustworthiness (61% recognize).
- **80%** of executives worry AI may threaten worker agency.
- **95%** concerned about accuracy of data from unregulated AI tools.
- **48%** of executives worry AI may degrade worker reasoning and slow development.
- **60%** of executives now regularly use AI for decisions; **85%** of leaders regret or question past decisions.
- **42%** say they're already using AI to monitor workforce signals (only 20% say great progress).
- **64%** consider managing AI's decision implications very important.
- **65%** of respondents believe their culture needs to change significantly.
- **34%** of organizations recognize culture as inhibiting AI transformation.
- **88%** say orchestrating people-skills-resources is important; only **7%** great progress.
- **28%** currently use dynamic teams; **59%** say it will be important.
- **85%** say developing adaptiveness is critical; only **7%** leading.
- Top impacts of change cited by workers: decreased well-being (**68%**), increased workload (**60%**), feeling less relevant (**58%**), decrease in employability (**56%**).
- **67%** of leaders say differentiation (not scale) is primary competitive edge.
- 50% of leaders say they prefer to shape strategy through frequent experimentation; **37%** prefer top-down.
- One-third have exited workers due to AI; **57%** of those have come to question that decision.

## 4. Deloitte — 2026 Gen Z and Millennial Survey

**Methodology:** 22,500+ respondents across 44 countries. Gen Zs born 1995-2007; millennials born 1983-1994.

**Headline findings:**
- Cost of living top concern for 5th consecutive year: **38% of Gen Zs** and **42% of millennials**.
- **55% of Gen Zs** and **52% of millennials** report delaying major life decisions (marriage, family, business, education).
- **47%** of both generations live paycheck to paycheck (down from 52% in 2025).
- **51% Gen Zs / 40% millennials** say they cannot afford to own a home.
- **44% Gen Zs / 45% millennials** prefer steady career progression; only **25% Gen Zs / 21% millennials** seek fast-paced growth.
- Only **6%** say achieving a leadership position is their top career priority.
- Top barriers to leadership: stress/burnout (**50% Gen Zs / 49% millennials**), excessive responsibility (**50% / 48%**), work-life balance (**41% / 46%**).
- **76% Gen Zs / 67% millennials** still interested in pursuing executive leadership at some point.
- **74%** of both use AI in their day-to-day work — **up sharply from 57% (Gen Zs) and 56% (millennials) in 2025**.
- AI use: identify learning/development (79%), seek career advice (72% Gen Z, 69% millennials), cope with stress (67% Gen Zs, 65% millennials).
- **30% Gen Zs / 31% millennials** believe their organization is NOT prepared for AI changes — **up from 20% last year**.
- **84%** of companies have not redesigned jobs around AI capabilities (Deloitte State of AI in the Enterprise reference).
- Mental health rated good/extremely good: **63% Gen Zs / 66% millennials** — up from 52% / 58% in 2025.
- **48% of Gen Zs / 45% of millennials** report feeling burned out.
- **40%** have rejected a project or potential employer based on personal ethics or beliefs.
- **96% Gen Zs / 97% millennials** say having a sense of purpose at work is important.
- **48%** of Gen Zs with workplace friendships plan to stay >5 years (vs **33%** without); **61% vs 43%** for millennials.

## 5. Deloitte — Beyond Productivity / Quantified Organization

**Methodology:** Survey of IT, data science, and data engineering professionals in North America + global executive interviews. (Note: this is a 2023-anchored Deloitte publication that's still being referenced in 2026.)

**Headline findings:**
- Data volumes growing **63% per month** on average in surveyed organizations.
- Organizations collect data from ~400 different sources.
- **1 in 3** medium/large companies adopted new worker-monitoring tools between pandemic start and late 2022.
- **79%** of employees willing to share data IF they get clear benefits in exchange.
- **90%** of employees surveyed willing to share data when the value exchange is explicit.
- **76%** of workers want their data to be portable; only ~half of business and HR leaders are aware of this expectation.
- Only **28%** of workers strongly agreed their leadership uses data responsibly.
- Only **30%** of employees comfortable with employers tracking worker data.
- **>50%** of workers report being uncomfortable with broad data collection without consent.
- ~**44% of teams** have a network structure that's solidly gender-mixed (Deloitte network analysis).
- **5.3%** efficiency gain achievable through redesigning team structures using data analytics.
- 15% sales lift achievable through customer-personality-matched experiences.

## 6. Aon — 2026 Human Capital Trends Study

**Methodology:** Conducted Nov 2025–Jan 2026. APAC had 1,107 respondents. Multi-country breakdown including Hong Kong, India, Malaysia, Singapore, China, Australia, etc.

**Headline findings (global):**
- **44%** of organizations have deployed AI; **29%** piloting; **8%** not actively using.
- **28%** of organizations have fully operational AI strategies for HR.
- Only **18%** report that most of their workforce has participated in AI reskilling/upskilling in the past 12 months — **the binding constraint on AI ROI**.
- **49%** identify HR as a top priority for AI deployment (the second-highest area).
- **76%** of employers agree hiring is increasingly difficult.
- **80%** of employers cite "operational efficiency" as a primary AI objective; **57%** have already automated routine tasks.
- **86%** agree AI will automate some tasks but existing roles will still be necessary.
- **88%** agree AI will create new opportunities and require new skills.
- Only **23%** agree AI will significantly replace jobs in their field.
- **84%** have confidence in their wellbeing strategies, yet **72%** of employees still report falling short on delivery.
- Only **38%** of organizations report high HR data maturity.
- Only **19%** of organizations have a clearly defined and well-understood EVP.
- **63%** of employees would be more loyal to an employer with mature pay transparency; only **22%** rate their organization's practices as mature.
- **84%** have confidence in equal pay for equal work policies, but only **14%** have policies supporting menopause/women's health.
- Only **35%** identify workforce upskilling as a primary AI priority.

**APAC-specific findings:**
- **Hong Kong:** AI deployment **60%** (vs 44% global); only **8%** can recruit and retain enough AI-skilled talent.
- **China:** AI deployment **38%** (slightly below global); **44%** report high data maturity (highest globally for APAC sample).
- **India:** Deployment **43%**; **48%** putting in upskilling effort; **39%** confident in AI talent retention.
- **Malaysia:** Deployment **21%** (well below global); only **10%** have a clear EVP; only **14%** mature pay transparency; only **5%** confident in AI skills retention.
- **Singapore:** (regional snapshots in country-specific pages).
- **Australia:** Deployment **48%**; **32%** putting in upskilling investment; **13%** mature pay transparency.

## 7. Aon — 2026 Global Medical Trend Rates Report

**Methodology:** Aon professionals across 100+ locations broker/administer medical plans. Trend rates = expected % increase in medical plan unit costs.

**Headline findings:**
- **2026 global gross medical trend: 9.8%**, continuing upward pressure from 9.3% in 2025.
- Two-thirds of countries expect higher trend rates in 2026 than 2025.
- **APAC**: gross trend **11%** for 2026 (slight increase from 11.1% in 2025 — second-highest region behind LAC).
- **LAC (Latin America)**: gross trend **11.3%** (up from 11.1% in 2025).
- **MEA (Middle East and Africa)**: **10.2%** (down 50bps from 10.7%).
- **North America**: **7.6%** in 2026 (vs 7.3% in 2025).
- **Europe**: **8.2%** (down from 8.9%); UK down to **5%** post-pandemic flattening.
- Top three cost drivers globally: **(1)** cardiovascular conditions, **(2)** cancer/tumor growth, **(3)** prescription drugs.
- **60%+ of countries** cite prescription drugs as a top driver.
- GLP-1 obesity drug premiums up significantly; **25%** of prescription costs go to GLP-1 medications in some markets.
- **86%** of countries report aggressive cost-containment measures (up from prior year).
- **70%** of multinational companies say medical-cost negotiation is the #1 strategic priority.

## 8. WTW — 2026 Global Medical Trends Survey

**Methodology:** Insurer perspective survey. Cross-region tracking 2024-2026.

**Headline findings:**
- **2026 global gross trend: 10.3%** (up from 10.0% in 2025 and 9.5% in 2024).
- **Asia Pacific: 14.0%** — highest of any region (up from 13.2% in 2025 and 11.8% in 2024).
- **Latin America: 11.9%** (up from 10.5% in 2025).

- **Europe: 8.2%** (stable from 8.3%).
- **56%** of insurers anticipate further increases in global medical cost trends.
- **55%** expect elevated levels to persist for more than three years.
- Top global cost driver: **new medical technologies (74% of insurers)**.
- Decline of public health systems: **52%** globally; **76%** in Europe (top driver there).
- Advancements in pharmaceuticals: **49%** globally.
- **Cancer cited as fastest-growing condition (48% globally)** and fastest-growing/most expensive diagnosis (57% globally).
- **Three in four insurers (74%)** have seen increased cancer incidence among <40-year-olds.
- **81% of insurers** report breast cancer rising fastest globally.
- **66%** of insurers globally expect GLP-1 obesity drug usage to increase; **78%** in Americas, **66%** APAC, **64%** Europe.
- **61%** of insurers globally offer behavioral health coverage; only **48%** in APAC, **30%** in MEA.
- **17%** of insurers report using AI in healthcare administration; expected to triple to **37%** in 2 years.
- Africa (43%) leads APAC (8%) in AI healthcare administration adoption.
- **81%** of insurers expect tariffs/trade policies to impact healthcare costs.
- **74%** of insurers identify contracted networks as the most important cost management tool.
- **76%** of insurers exclude war-related risks; **74%** gender-reaffirming care; **70%** alcoholism/drug use; **66%** family planning/fertility.

**Headline findings:**
- **SEA GDP: US$4.12 trillion in 2024**, forecast 4.1% annual growth.
- **380 million under-35 population** in SEA — surpasses entire US population.
- **930 million mobile connections** in SEA.
- **70 million MSMEs** in SEA = ~97% of workforce.

- **<3%** of SEA respondents cite leadership sponsorship as a barrier (leaders are bought in).
- **AI high performers in SEA** (companies with >US$250M revenue, attributing >5% EBIT to AI) are **3.5x more likely** to expect AI to transform their business in 3 years.
- AI high performers: **8 in 10** see strong impact already.
- ~**85% of digital-solutions value** delivered through AI per PETRONAS.
- **74%** of high performers have fundamentally redesigned workflows after deploying AI.

## 11. PwC — 29th Global CEO Survey 2026

**Methodology:** 4,454 CEOs surveyed in 95 countries/territories, Sept 30 2025–Jan 2026. Theme: "Leading through uncertainty in the age of AI."

**Headline findings:**
- **56%** of CEOs say their AI investments have produced neither revenue growth nor cost reduction.
- **30%** report increased revenue from AI; **26%** report lower costs.
- Only **12%** report BOTH revenue growth and cost savings from AI — the "vanguard."
- Vanguard companies apply AI broadly across business areas (**44%**) vs only **17%** of others.
- CEO revenue-growth confidence: **30%** very/extremely confident (down from **38%** prior year and peak **56%** in 2022).
- **84%** of CEOs say their company is at least moderately affected by geopolitical risk.
- **66%** experienced stakeholder trust concerns in past 12 months.
- Trust concerns include data privacy (**38%**), demands for transparency (**38%**), responsible AI (**37%**), AI-related decisions (**36%**).
- **42%** competing in new sectors over past 5 years; **44%** plan deals outside existing sector in next 3 years.
- **47%** of CEO time spent on <1-year horizons; only **16%** on >5-year horizons.
- Private equity-backed CEOs spend **57%** of time on short-term issues; public-company CEOs only **39%**.
- **29%** expect tariffs to reduce profit margins; **60%** expect little to no change.
- **31%** highly/extremely exposed to cyber risk in year ahead (up from **24%** prior year, **21%** two years ago).
- **20%** of CEOs say their company is highly/extremely exposed to climate-related risks.
- Only **1 in 4** CEOs incorporate AI into supply chain (24%), product design (24%), or M&A (20%) decisions to a large or very large extent.
- US tops international investment destinations (**35%**); India jumped from **7%** to **13%** year-over-year.
- "Dynamic" companies (the ~85% that plan major moves) growing faster than "cautious" companies (~15% planning no major acquisitions).

## 16. ILO — Employment and Social Trends 2026

**Methodology:** ILO World of Work Series flagship, 96 pages. Covers global, regional, and country-level labor market projections to 2026/2027.

**Global headline findings:**
- Global unemployment projected at **4.9%** in 2026 (stable from 2025) — **186 million unemployed**.
- **Jobs gap (broader underutilization measure): 408 million** in 2026, up from ~403 million in 2025.
- **2.1 billion workers globally in informal employment** in 2026.
- **284 million workers in extreme poverty** (less than US$3/day).
- Working poverty improvement has slowed to half the pace of the previous decade (3.1 percentage points over 10 years vs. 6.2 prior decade).
- Gender employment gap: **14.3 percentage points** — women's labor force participation lags men by this margin globally.
- Gender jobs gap rate: **4.3 percentage points** in 2026.
- Youth unemployment: **12.3%** globally, having stabilized after declining since 2020.
- **465 million jobs in 80 countries** were linked to global trade in 2024 — trade is **34.1%** of manufacturing employment and **22.9%** of market services employment.
- Global trade uncertainty index reached **unprecedented levels** in first half of 2025.
- Real services trade grew **85% since 2005** (vs **33%** for goods).
- Remittances: US$685 billion to developing countries in 2024 (now larger than FDI + ODA combined).

**Asia and Pacific findings:**
- Asia/Pacific employed: ~**1.85 billion** people in 2025; ~**56%** of global employed population by 2030.
- Regional unemployment: **4.1%** in 2025 (stable).
- **65.4% of employment in Asia/Pacific is informal** — ~330 million informal workers.
- **48.6%** services employment share in Asia (2022), up from **35.9%** in 1995.
- China: ~**15 million manufacturing jobs lost** in past decade.
- China: gig economy workers reach up to **80%** of workforce in some industries during peak seasons.
- **78%** of Asia/Pacific wealth derived from natural capital.
- Asia/Pacific accounts for ~**60% of global greenhouse gas emissions**.
- Asia/Pacific generates **46%** of world's solar/wind energy.
- LinkedIn data shows green-jobs hiring share rising in Asia.
- **278 million** trade-linked jobs in Asia/Pacific — the largest share globally.
- **Trade cost shock simulation** (ILO): SEA wages projected to fall **0.3% per year**; Southern Asia wages fall **0.45% per year**; both lasting 5 years.

**Other regional notes:**
- Sub-Saharan Africa: **46% of population** lives on less than US$3/day (2024); **140 million** food-insecure.
- Africa labor force projected to grow from ~**230 million** (1995) to **610 million** (2030) — biggest growth globally.
- Working poverty in Africa: still **31%** extreme poverty rate of employed population.
- Sectoral employment shifts globally **halved** over past decade — economic restructuring slowing.

## 18. ILO — Working on a Warmer Planet (foundational 2019 study; still primary referenced source)

**Methodology:** ILO global modeling using HadGEM2 + GFDL-ESM2M climate models under RCP2.6 (limiting warming to 1.5°C). Country-level projections for 1995, 2025, 2030. **Note: published 2019 but still the primary cited source for 2026-era heat-labor productivity stats.**

**Headline findings:**
- By 2030, **2.2% of total working hours globally lost to heat stress** = equivalent of **80 million full-time jobs**.
- Under pessimistic scenario, hours lost rise to **3.8%** = **136 million full-time jobs**.
- Estimated economic loss from heat stress at work: **US$280 billion in 1995 → US$2,400 billion in 2030**.
- ~**2.4 billion workers globally** exposed to excessive heat at work.
- **60% of working hours lost** projected to be in agriculture (subsistence agriculture especially).
- **90%+** of working hours lost in Central/Eastern Africa = agricultural.
- **Southern Asia: 5.3%** of working hours lost (43M full-time jobs equivalent) — highest globally.
- **Western Africa: 4.8%** working hours lost.
- **India alone: equivalent of 34 million full-time jobs lost** in 2030.
- Most heat-affected countries (Asia/Pacific top 10): India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Vietnam, Cambodia, Philippines, Indonesia, Myanmar, Nepal, Sri Lanka.
- **Australia: equivalent of 147,000 full-time jobs lost** in 2030 (small % but high absolute).
- Cabo Verde, Mauritania, Burkina Faso, Niger, Mali among highest impacted in Africa.
- Heatwaves projected to double in frequency between 2000 and 2030 in many regions.
- **Heat-related migration:** Southern Asia projected net out-migration; high-heat-stress regions losing population to cooler ones.
- One-degree (1°C) temperature increase causes **~2% productivity loss** in brickmaking (India study).
- Construction = **40% of total Arab States working hours lost** to heat (driven by GCC).
- Urban Heat Island effect adds further losses (up to ~5.6% city GDP by end of century in some studies).
- ILO Hygiene Convention No. 120 and OSH frameworks discussed as policy levers.
