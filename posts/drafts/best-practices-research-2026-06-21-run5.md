- **LEAD FIRM:** Deloitte (Big-3, 0-lead priority slot filled)
- **Cross-firm mentions:** 3 — Deloitte (T1), WEF (T1), Gartner (T2)
- **Tier-1 count:** 2 (Deloitte + WEF) — clears the bar
- **Specific reports:**
  - Deloitte — 2026 Global Human Capital Trends, worker-data/trust chapter ("AI is eroding authorship, agency and the authenticity of workforce data… shift from cybersecurity to *disinformation security*") (Feb 2026) — https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/talent/human-capital-trends.html `[search-only]`
  - WEF — Global Risks Report 2026 + "How cognitive manipulation and AI will shape disinformation in 2026" (Jan/Mar 2026): misinformation/disinformation ranks #2 in the two-year risk outlook; adverse-AI outcomes posted the single largest ranking jump; employment fraud set to escalate as generative AI produces deepfake candidates who pass interviews in real time — https://www.weforum.org/stories/2026/03/how-cognitive-manipulation-and-ai-will-shape-disinformation-in-2026/ `[search-only]`
  - Gartner — 2026 hiring survey: 59% of hiring managers suspect candidates of using AI to misrepresent themselves; projection that by 2028 one in four candidate profiles worldwide will be fake (URL not confirmed) `[search-only]` `[unverified]`
- **Why it's a fresh Deloitte chapter:** This is NOT dedup #5 (decisions-that-echo / AI governance) or #13 (change management). It is Deloitte's distinct worker-data/trust thesis — the integrity of the data HR itself runs on. No overlap with any of the 15 windowed themes.
- **Core claim (synthesized):** AI has quietly attacked the reliability of the data HR depends on. Exaggerated and synthetic CVs, deepfake interviewees, cloned voices and "workslop" are contaminating hiring pipelines and the models built on top of them. Deloitte argues the fix is a posture shift from cybersecurity to "disinformation security" — identity authentication, output provenance/lineage, red-teaming and recruiter training in spotting manipulation. WEF frames the same force as a top-tier enterprise risk; Gartner quantifies how far into the funnel it already reaches.
- **Most quotable stat:** 59% of hiring managers already suspect a candidate used AI to misrepresent themselves (Gartner, 2026). `[search-only]` `[unverified]` Backup (projection): 1 in 4 candidate profiles will be fake by 2028 (Gartner).
- **Scroll-stopping hook (no em-dash, no inversion):** "Six in ten of your hiring managers already believe a candidate has lied to them using AI."
- **Suggested post angle:** The résumé, the reference, the interview video — HR's core evidence is now forgeable. Treat candidate data like a security surface, not a formality.

## Theme 2 — The AI diffusion gap (near-universal adoption, payoff concentrated in a fifth of firms)
- **LEAD FIRM:** Conference Board (Tier 2, 0-lead priority slot filled)
- **Cross-firm mentions:** 4 — Conference Board (T2), McKinsey (T1), PwC (T2), BCG (T1)
- **Tier-1 count:** 2 (McKinsey + BCG) — clears the bar
- **Specific reports:**
  - Conference Board — "Europe's AI Productivity Paradox: Rising Uptake, Shallow Diffusion" (27 May 2026) + "Transforming Organizations for AI: Critical Factors for AI Success" (2026) — https://www.conference-board.org/publications/transforming-organizations-for-AI-critical-factors-for-AI-success `[search-only]`
  - McKinsey — "Where AI will create value, and where it won't" (2026): adoption and capex are accelerating but "sustained impact on performance is elusive"; most deployments "accelerate existing work" while "largely preserving underlying workflows" — gains only arrive when work is redesigned around AI — https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/where-ai-will-create-value-and-where-it-wont `[search-only]`
  - PwC — 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer: the top 20% most AI-exposed companies posted ~163% labour-productivity growth vs 2018, nearly five times the average AI-exposed firm; most-exposed sectors grew productivity 34% vs 24% for least-exposed — https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2026/pwc-2026-ai-jobs-barometer.html `[search-only]`
  - BCG — 2026 AI@Work research (corroborates adoption-without-redesign / value not captured) `[search-only]`
- **Dedup basis (recycled, aged-out parent "AI value-capture," 2026-05):** Distinct from #11 (BCG time-reinvestment, a worker-level saved-time metric) and #10 (McKinsey Shift #1 speed). This theme is the ORG/MACRO diffusion level — why fleet-wide adoption is not showing up in fleet-wide productivity. Fresh 2026 sub-evidence: Conference Board's May-2026 "shallow diffusion" cut + McKinsey's 2026 value-vs-no-value framing + PwC's 163%-concentration number.
- **Core claim (synthesized):** Almost every large employer now runs AI, yet the measurable productivity gain concentrates in a narrow band of firms — the ones that redesigned workflows rather than bolting AI onto old processes. Conference Board calls it rising uptake with shallow diffusion; McKinsey calls it a payoff that is real but conditional; PwC shows the winners pulling multiples ahead. The differentiator is not access to the tool, it is willingness to rebuild the work around it.
- **Most quotable stat:** The top 20% most AI-exposed companies saw ~163% labour-productivity growth since 2018, roughly five times the average AI-exposed firm (PwC, 2026). `[search-only]`
- **Scroll-stopping hook (no em-dash, no inversion):** "Nearly every big employer bought AI last year. The productivity gains showed up in about one company out of five."
- **Suggested post angle:** Buying AI is table stakes and it changes nothing on its own. The firms seeing returns tore up the workflow first; the rest are paying for software that preserves the old process.

## Theme 3 — Flat raise budgets, quietly redirected to AI and critical skills
- **LEAD FIRM:** WTW (Tier 1, non-Big-3, not Mercer)
- **Cross-firm mentions:** 3 — WTW (T1), Mercer (T1), PwC (T2)
- **Tier-1 count:** 2 (WTW + Mercer) — clears the bar
- **Specific reports:**
  - WTW — 2026 Salary Budget Planning Survey (35,000+ responses, 1,876 orgs): 2026 US budgets flat at 3.4%; 62% made no change, only 6% increasing, but 21% now plan to DECREASE budgets; remaining dollars steered to retain critical/scarce-skill talent and fix pay compression rather than spread evenly — https://www.wtwco.com/en-us/news/2026/01/salary-budgets-have-stabilised-as-employers-focus-on-pay-strategy-for-2026 `[search-only]`
  - WTW — "US pulls further ahead on AI pay" (May 2026): AI/digital-talent pay diverging sharply by market; ML roles top $170k US vs ~$122k Germany, ~$100k UK — https://www.wtwco.com/en-us/news/2026/05/us-pulls-further-ahead-on-ai-pay-as-europe-reshuffles-and-emerging-markets-accelerate `[search-only]`
  - Mercer — Global Talent Trends 2026 (comp/rewards): transparency and equity as foundations; strategic, differentiated reward allocation — https://www.mercer.com/insights/people-strategy/future-of-work/global-talent-trends/ `[search-only]`
  - PwC — 2026 Global AI Jobs Barometer: average wage premium for AI-skilled workers hit 62%, up from 57% a year earlier (as high as 118% in consumer markets) — https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2026/pwc-2026-ai-jobs-barometer.html `[search-only]`
- **Dedup basis (recycled, aged-out parent "skills-based planning"):** Not #7 (EU pay transparency execution) or #8 (benefits affordability). This is the compensation-differentiation angle — a shrinking pot allocated by skill. Fresh 2026 sub-evidence: WTW SBP-2026 (21% cutting budgets) + PwC 2026 barometer (62% AI premium).
- **Core claim (synthesized):** Total raise budgets are barely moving (3.4% flat), and more employers are cutting than raising them. But the money that remains is being concentrated, not sprinkled — routed to scarce-skill and AI-capable talent while everyone else's increase thins out. The clearest signal is the AI wage premium: PwC now puts it at 62%, and it is still climbing. Pay is quietly becoming skills-based whether or not the org has a formal skills-based-pay program.
- **Most quotable stat:** The wage premium for AI-skilled workers reached 62% in 2026, up from 57% a year earlier (PwC). `[search-only]` Lead-firm anchor: 21% of employers plan to CUT their 2026 pay budgets vs 6% raising them (WTW). `[search-only]`
- **Scroll-stopping hook (no em-dash, no inversion):** "One in five employers is shrinking its raise budget this year while quietly paying a 62% premium for AI skills."
- **Suggested post angle:** The flat 3.4% headline hides the real move: the raise pool is being redistributed toward a small group of scarce-skill people. If your comp philosophy still spreads increases evenly, you are already funding your competitors' poaching.
