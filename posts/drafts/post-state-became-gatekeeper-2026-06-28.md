Same Friday — June 26 — two frontier AI models shipped under matched federal-review choreography.

OpenAI released GPT-5.6 (Sol / Terra / Luna) to ~20 US-government-approved organizations. API and Codex only. No ChatGPT, no waitlist. Anthropic released Mythos 5 to 100+ US institutions under the same gatekept-preview pattern, cleared by the same government on the same day.

This is the June 2 Executive Order on pre-release model evaluation showing up as operational reality. Not a one-off review for one lab. A shared release shape across the frontier.

The mirror moves were visible all week:

— The same Senate Banking Committee that Anthropic wrote to about Alibaba/Qwen-linked actors running ~25,000 fake accounts for 28.8 million Claude exchanges is now preparing sanctions on Chinese AI rivals.

— CISA — the same federal layer now vetting frontier models — issued an emergency directive on Check Point VPN zero-day CVE-2026-50751. Different intervention mode, same operator.

— NVIDIA at ISC announced 35 new European AI supercomputers in development. The EU is treating sovereign infrastructure count as an industrial-policy unit — the European mirror of the US gatekeeping move.

Two reads I'm working through:

1. Procurement-side: frontier-model contracts now route through federal-review gates. Multi-week clearance lag is the new baseline assumption for any Sol- or Mythos-class workload.

2. Architecture-side: if releases are slow and tiered, inference economics have to compress. That's the link between the gatekeeping pattern and Jalapeño's ~50% inference-cost cut — same week, same logic. Cheaper compute makes the slower release survivable.

The frontier didn't disappear behind a federal door. It just learned to walk through one.
